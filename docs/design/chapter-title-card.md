# Chapter Title Card

## Scope

Every chapter video opens with a short title card identifying the book +
chapter (in the target/heart language) and the language name, so a viewer
can immediately see what the video covers and what language it's in.
Decided with Dan 2026-09-10 (Mark 2 healing-sequence pass): **opening card
only** — no persistent on-screen mark for the rest of the runtime. Two
options were mocked up (card-only vs. card + a persistent bottom-left
corner mark surviving the whole video); Dan chose card-only to keep the
rest of the runtime clean.

## Spec

- **Duration**: the approved hold for that chapter, at the very start before
  the source performance. Mark 3 used 2.5 seconds; the approved Mark 4
  treatment uses 4 seconds. A language edition retains its selected chapter's
  reviewed opening timing rather than applying a series-wide default.
- **Background**: the chapter's own clean establishing shot. The approved
  Mark 4 lake card uses the undarkened sunrise frame
  `generated/editorial/mark4-intro/establishing-r1.png`. New text is composed
  over this clean frame, never over the Hatam-lettered donor card.
- **Title text**: book + chapter number in the target language's own
  spelling, centered in white Georgia Bold with a soft shadow. The Mark 4
  reference raster has its title around 28–37% of frame height and uses a
  font size about 9.2% of frame height.
- **Language label**: the target language's own name, centered below the
  title in smaller gold Arial. The approved Mark 4 lake card has no short
  white separator rule or artificial spaces between its label letters.
- **No AI-rendered text**: this card is a real composited graphics overlay
  built with a font-rendering tool (e.g. Pillow), never generated by the
  image/video providers — every provider prompt in this pipeline already
  forbids in-scene text/labels (see `CASTING_ANCHOR` in the keyframe
  generation scripts), so the title card is assembled after the fact, not
  requested from a shot's own generation.
- **Per-chapter parameters**: verified target-language book/chapter and
  language name, the selected chapter's clean establishing background, and
  its approved title duration. The white serif title, smaller language label,
  rule, and text placement follow the Mark 4 treatment for the first Meyah
  edition and Dan's requested Mark 5 title. The source spelling is stored
  separately from uppercase series presentation; diacritics are retained.
  A target-language title is never drawn over a frame with source-language
  letters burned in.

## Reference implementation

`src/pipeline/assembly/chapter-title-card.ts` composes a title over a verified
title-free establishing frame and creates a new, exclusive clip at the approved
duration. `scripts/build-chapter-title-card.ts` remains the CLI wrapper.
Opening media is selected through the existing editorial-beat review and
assembly path; this helper alone never approves or inserts a title into a cut.
The title clip is silent/ambient under it; narration starts with the source
performance, per the existing "narrator must never go silent" rule, which
applies after the story begins.
